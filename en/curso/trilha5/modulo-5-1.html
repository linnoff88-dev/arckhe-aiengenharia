MODULE 5.1

🔄 Think in systems, not commands

The invisible bottleneck case: ten excellent prompts, an organized library—and the work still gets stuck because every step depends on you remembering to do it. This module teaches you to see what’s missing: the process that connects the pieces.

➡️ From isolated command to workflow

A standalone command asks: "what does the AI do?" A workflow asks: "how do the steps connect to produce a predictable result?" The difference is that the workflow doesn’t depend on you being in the middle, remembering what comes next.

🧩 The difference that changes everything

Standalone command: you ask → AI responds → you decide what to do. It’s all in your head, all in the moment.

Workflow: the process decides what comes next. You define the rules once — the system applies them every time.

💡 Practical Tip

To turn a habit into a workflow, ask: "if I had to teach someone to do this without them asking me anything, what would the steps be?" The answer to this question is your workflow. Write it down on paper before opening any tool.

2

🤖 What AI does in a system

AI isn’t an oracle that solves everything with one question. In a system, it takes on specific roles at specific stages. Understanding these roles is what lets you distribute the work intelligently.

✓ What AI does well in a system

  • ✓Interprets: turns raw input into something structured
  • ✓Generates a proposal: produces a first draft against the criteria
  • ✓Measures: compare the output against an explicit rubric
  • ✓Compare: two versions side by side with criteria
  • ✓Executes parts: handles one part of the process while another step is done by a person

✗ What NOT to delegate to AI in the system

  • ✗Final decision: publish, send, sign — always with a human
  • ✗Contextual judgment: "makes sense for client X" requires knowledge she doesn’t have
  • ✗Legal responsibility: no AI system signs on your behalf
  • ✗Define criteria: what "good" means is your choice — not the AI's

AI ROLES BY BOX — EXAMPLE OF AN ANALYSIS SYSTEM

Box 1 — Interpretation: "Read this document and extract the 5 main points as bullet points."
Box 2 — Analysis: "Based on the 5 points, identify risks and opportunities."
Box 3 — Proposal: "Suggest 3 priority actions, ranked by impact × effort."
Box 4 — Evaluation: "Does each action meet criteria X, Y, Z? Point out what doesn't."
↳ human decides: approve, adjust, or discard
3

✏️ Designing the first workflow

The most important exercise in the module. Take a process you already do—creating a post, writing a report, creating a lesson—and put it on paper in boxes. Before using any tool.

1

List the steps you already take

Chosen process: creating a social media post

You already do this mentally: think about the topic, choose the angle, draft, review, adjust the length, publish. Write each step on a sticky note (real or mental).

2

Mark: human or AI?

For each step, decide who executes it

Human: choose the topic, approve the tone, publish. AI: structure the draft, write, review against criteria. Mark them with a different color or symbol on paper.

3

Connect the boxes with arrows

The workflow starts to take shape

Where does the AI receive something from a person? Where does it deliver something back? The arrows reveal the handoffs—and also where the process usually gets stuck (steps without an incoming arrow are bottlenecks).

4

Add the return arrow

The loop closes the system

From the Evaluation box, draw an arrow back to the previous box when the result doesn’t pass. This cyan arrow is what turns a linear sequence into a system that improves.

💡 Practical Tip

Your first workflow doesn’t need to be perfect. The draft will reveal bottlenecks you didn’t know existed — steps where the process always depends on you being there. These are the points the architecture will solve.

4

🔀 Decision points and branches

Not every workflow is a straight line. Sometimes the process branches: if A, then it goes in box X; if B, it goes in box Y. Mapping these forks turns implicit judgment into an explicit rule.

EXAMPLES OF DECISION POINTS IN COMMON FLOWS

Evaluated post → Appropriate tone?
✓ yes → proceed to publication
✗ no → go back to rewriting with tone instructions

Proposal generated → Is the client new or returning?
→ new: add an introduction section
→ recurring: skip the introduction, go straight to the scope

Analysis complete → Risk level?
→ low: approve automatically
→ high: route for human review

📊 Why branches matter

  • • Turn rules that were in your head into documented logic
  • • Let the system handle different cases without you having to step in each time
  • • They are the foundation of systems that scale — more cases, same logic

🧩 How to design

  • • Use a different shape (diamond or triangle) for decision points
  • • Write the condition as a question: “approved?”
  • • Put “yes” and “no” (or the relevant values) on the outgoing arrows
5

🤝 Where the human steps in — AI × human handoff

A system without clear handoffs is an automation that fails silently or a manual task that never gets done. The handoff is the point where the baton changes hands — and it needs to be explicit in both directions.

Human → AI (you provide input to the system)

  • →Provides the topic, brief, or initial context
  • →Define the quality criteria before running it
  • →Injects the knowledge base (T3 baseline briefing)
  • →Decides which path to take at high-stakes decision points

AI → Human (the system delivers it to you)

  • ←Delivers a draft for approval or adjustment
  • ←Presents the risk analysis for a human decision
  • ←Flags when the result doesn’t meet the criteria
  • ←Delivers the final product for publication/sending (always a human action)

⚡ Practical handoff rule

Label each box in your workflow with AI or H (human). If you have 3 AI boxes in a row without any H in the middle, check: is there any point where your judgment should come in? A system without human involvement isn’t efficient—it’s risky.

6

🔁 The loop logic: Think → Act → Evaluate → Adjust → Act

The invisible bottleneck in the anchor case existed because there was no loop. AI generated—and stopped. With the loop, the system evaluates its own output and corrects itself until you reach the criterion you defined.

THE THINK → ACT → EVALUATE → ADJUST → ACT LOOP — IN PROMPTS

[THINK — you decide before starting]
Criteria: the post needs a hook in the 1st line, ≤ 150 words, and 1 clear CTA.

[ACT — AI generates]
"Write a post about [topic] following the criteria above."

[EVALUATE — AI evaluates its own result]
"Now analyze the post it generated: does it meet the 3 criteria? For each one, answer yes/no and why."

[ADJUST → ACT — if there was a "no"]
"Rewrite the post to fix the points that didn't meet the criteria. Don't change what was already correct."

Think

Criteria defined beforehand — not afterward. Quality isn’t a post-hoc judgment.

Act

AI executes based on the criteria. The first draft is rarely the final one.

Evaluate

Compares the output against a criterion. AI self-evaluates better when you’ve provided the rubric.

Adjust

Fix only what failed — don't start from scratch. The loop closes.

7

🌐 Overview of tools — no intimidation, no dependency

This track is 100% no-code. But there's a horizon beyond chat—and learning about it without getting intimidated is part of the architecture layer. You don’t need these tools right now. But you’ll want to know they exist.

📊 The complexity spectrum—where you are now is enough

Level 1 (now): Prompts chained together in a chat. Flow run manually. Works today, with no setup.
Level 2 (later): No-code tools (n8n, Make, Zapier): the workflow runs automatically. Requires learning the interface.
Level 3 (when it makes sense): AI agents: multiple AIs collaborating in different roles. High complexity, high power.

🧩 Why starting at Level 1 is an advantage, not a limitation

Those who understand the concepts of flow, handoff, and loop can learn any tool in hours—because the tool implements what they already have in mind.

Those who start with the tool without understanding the concept become dependent on its settings and get lost when the tool changes.

⚡ Slow User Shortcut

Your simplest system starts now, in chat, with 3 prompts in sequence. When you want to automate, the workflow will already be documented—just map it to your chosen tool. You’re not behind. You’re building the foundation.
